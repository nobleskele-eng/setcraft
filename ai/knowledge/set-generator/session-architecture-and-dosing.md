# Start from one primary purpose

Every session may contain several ingredients, but one or two outcomes should control the design. Examples include aerobic durability, race-pace repeatability, maximal-speed quality, turn execution under controlled fatigue, breaststroke timing, or recovery.

Avoid a session that stacks unrelated hard blocks merely because they sound advanced.

# Session sequence

A flexible structure is:

1. arrival and general preparation;
2. movement and technical preparation;
3. neural skill, start, turn, or speed work when freshness matters;
4. primary set;
5. secondary support set;
6. recovery and easy skill integration;
7. debrief and recorded evidence.

Sequence can change. A short pre-fatigue block may be intentional, but its purpose must be explicit.

# Dose variables

The training dose is not distance alone. SetCraft should expose:

- total distance and high-intensity distance;
- repeat duration and count;
- work-to-rest pattern or send-off;
- target speed or effort;
- stroke and equipment;
- technical demand;
- success and stop rules;
- location in the week;
- athlete level and entered restrictions.

# Intensity language

Prefer directly observable targets:

- time or pace band;
- percentage of a verified test or race pace when the method is defined;
- repetition consistency;
- RPE paired with pace and technique;
- critical-speed or threshold zone only when based on an appropriate protocol;
- technical outcome at a stated speed.

Do not assign a blood-lactate value unless a real measurement protocol exists. Labels such as aerobic, threshold, VO2-oriented, race pace, lactate production, or lactate tolerance are programming descriptions, not measured athlete states.

# Broad training families

## Aerobic skill and extensive work

Purpose: accumulate repeatable swimming while preserving mechanics and sustainable control.

Typical design features:

- longer total work;
- low to moderate intensity;
- stable technique and breathing;
- short to moderate rest consistent with the lane's actual completion time;
- simple quality controls such as even pace, stroke-count band, or legal turns.

## Threshold or critical-speed-oriented work

Purpose: develop sustainable high aerobic speed around a protocol-derived target.

Requirements:

- a defined test or recent reliable performance;
- repeat durations long enough to express the target;
- recovery that permits a meaningful accumulated duration without turning the set into sprint work;
- a pace window and a stop or reset rule.

Critical speed and lactate threshold are not interchangeable. State the protocol used.

## Aerobic power / VO2-oriented intervals

Purpose: accumulate quality time at high aerobic demand.

Use repeat duration, recovery, and total quality volume that match the athlete's level. Technique failure and large pace loss indicate that more work is not automatically better.

## Race-pace repeatability

Purpose: reproduce event-specific speed, skill, and decision cues across controlled repetitions.

Use verified target splits, meaningful race segments, sufficient recovery, and an explicit success count. If the swimmer cannot reach the target without distorting mechanics, adjust distance, recovery, or target rather than calling the set completed.

## Maximal speed and power

Purpose: preserve very high velocity and start/turn quality.

Use short efforts, generous recovery, low traffic, and strict quality control. Stop the maximal-speed block when velocity or execution declines beyond the coach's limit.

## Recovery

Purpose: support restoration, movement quality, and low-stress technical rehearsal.

Keep intensity genuinely low. Do not hide a second conditioning set inside the recovery label.

# Progression

Change one main variable at a time where practical:

- repetitions;
- repeat distance;
- pace;
- recovery;
- technical complexity;
- equipment;
- number of successful rounds;
- transfer from drill to swim.

Progress only when the athlete or lane meets the current success criteria consistently.

# Taper and competition preparation

Evidence supports reduced volume with maintained quality as a broad taper principle, but the exact reduction, duration, and frequency are individual. Preserve familiar race skills, avoid last-minute novelty, and monitor response. SetCraft should draft options, not guarantee a percentage performance gain.
