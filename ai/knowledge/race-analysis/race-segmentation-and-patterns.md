# Segment before interpreting

Use the definitions supplied by LaneLab. A race may be described through:

- start phase to a fixed distance;
- free-swimming segments;
- turn-in, wall, underwater, breakout, and turn-out phases;
- cumulative checkpoints;
- finish segment;
- stroke rate, stroke count, distance per cycle, or velocity when measured consistently.

Do not compare a 15 m start time with a head-at-15 m measurement if the reference uses another body landmark.

# Split terminology

- **Cumulative split:** elapsed time from the start to a checkpoint.
- **Segment split:** time between two checkpoints.
- **Positive split:** later comparable segment is slower.
- **Negative split:** later comparable segment is faster.
- **Even split:** comparable segments are similar within a stated tolerance.
- **Modeled checkpoint:** estimated rather than directly measured.

Positive, negative, or even is a description—not automatically good or bad. Interpret relative to the event, stroke, course, intended strategy, and athlete.

# 50 analysis

Prioritize:

- start and reaction data only when measured;
- 5/10/15 m segment definition;
- breakout location and velocity;
- free-swimming speed and stroke mechanics;
- breath pattern observation if available;
- finish timing and last-cycle decision.

Do not infer start quality from final time alone.

# 100 analysis

Prioritize:

- start-to-breakout;
- first-length or first-50 control;
- turn segment in short course or mid-race turn in long course;
- third-quarter speed/technique change;
- final segment and finish.

A large second-half drop may have several explanations: opening speed, technical disruption, turn loss, breathing pattern, race plan, or conditioning. Splits alone do not identify the cause.

# 200 analysis

Prioritize:

- first-50 control relative to the intended plan;
- middle-100 stability;
- third-50 transition;
- ability to preserve mechanics and tempo into the final 50;
- turn contribution and course differences.

Do not label the first 50 "too slow" merely because it is the slowest/fastest pattern without comparing to the supplied plan and matched references.

# 400/500 analysis

Look for:

- early settling cost;
- middle-segment variance;
- turn consistency;
- planned or unplanned pace changes;
- final-quarter reserve;
- stroke metric stability when available.

# 800/1000 and 1500/1650 analysis

Use rolling groups of splits to avoid overreacting to one length. Examine:

- early pacing and settling;
- mid-race stability;
- course-specific turns;
- response to pace changes;
- final build;
- missing or noisy splits.

If lap data is hand-entered or incomplete, reduce confidence.

# IM analysis

Separate:

- within-stroke performance;
- legal transitions;
- turn segment;
- pace distribution by stroke;
- final freestyle after accumulated mixed-stroke demand.

Do not compare raw split length across strokes as if speed demands were identical.

# Course effects

- SCY and SCM contain more wall events than LCM over comparable event distances.
- LCM has longer uninterrupted free-swimming segments.
- Reference races must match course or carry an explicit planning-conversion label.
- Never call an SCY benchmark a world record.

# Reference-race use

Prefer comparisons matched on:

- course and event;
- competition category;
- overall time or performance level;
- checkpoint availability;
- race shape;
- source reliability.

Explain both similarity and mismatch. A world-class race can illustrate a shape but may be a poor direct prescription for an age-group swimmer.
