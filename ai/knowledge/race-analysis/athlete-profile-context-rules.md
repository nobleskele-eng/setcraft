# Purpose

Athlete-profile data can help choose questions, but it must not manufacture causes. SetCraft may use context only to prioritize coach checks.

# Ratings from 1 to 10

Self-ratings for starts, turns, endurance, speed, technique, mobility, strength, or lactate tolerance are subjective. They may indicate what the athlete perceives, but they are not measured performance or physiology.

Good use:

"The athlete rated turns 4/10, so compare the measured turn segment and video before prioritizing turn work."

Bad use:

"The 4/10 rating proves weak leg power."

# Measured profile values

Use a value only with its protocol, date, units, and conditions. Examples:

- countermovement jump height;
- pull-up or strength result;
- measured blood lactate sample series;
- mobility assessment;
- critical-speed test;
- timed start or turn segment.

Do not compare values collected with different protocols as if identical.

# Age and training age

Age can guide communication and development context. It cannot determine talent, readiness, ideal volume, or expected body type. Training age and current technical competence may matter more for task selection.

# Height and mass

Treat as descriptive context only when relevant and consented. Do not infer body composition, health, event destiny, or technique from height or mass. Population associations do not justify individual judgments.

# Restrictions

Coach-entered restrictions are hard programming constraints. Race analysis may identify a question for the coach, but it must not diagnose, clear, or prescribe rehabilitation.

# Combining profile and race evidence

Use this order:

1. measured race evidence;
2. deterministic SetCraft calculations;
3. matched official references;
4. measured profile data with protocol;
5. coach observation;
6. self-rating.

If a lower layer conflicts with a higher one, describe the conflict and recommend verification.
