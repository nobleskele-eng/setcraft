# Three labels LaneLab must keep separate

- **Measured:** directly recorded with a named method, unit, date, and protocol.
- **Derived:** calculated by LaneLab from measured or official inputs using a disclosed formula.
- **Modeled:** estimated from a model, interpolation, course conversion, or assumed race shape.

Never relabel modeled data as measured. Never infer a measured physiological value from performance, appearance, body size, or a self-rating.

# Minimum useful metadata

For a time, split, tempo, stroke count, strength result, lactate sample, critical-speed test, or video observation, preserve:

1. athlete or anonymized athlete ID;
2. date and pool course;
3. event, stroke, distance, and start type;
4. method and device;
5. units and precision;
6. whether the value is measured, derived, or modeled;
7. relevant protocol details;
8. reviewer or recorder;
9. source link or file when applicable.

# Confidence ladder

- **High confidence:** official result or repeatable direct measurement with clear protocol.
- **Moderate confidence:** reliable coach timing/video observation or derived metric with complete inputs.
- **Low confidence:** modeled checkpoint, single informal trial, incomplete video, or ambiguous course.
- **Context only:** self-rating, memory, anecdote, unverified social post, or comparison without matched conditions.

Confidence applies to the evidence, not the athlete. A low-confidence observation is not a low-confidence swimmer.

# Observation before explanation

Use this order:

1. **Observation:** what changed and how it was measured.
2. **Comparison:** against the athlete's own baseline, target, or a matched reference.
3. **Possible interpretations:** more than one when the evidence permits.
4. **Discriminating check:** the next observation or set that would separate those explanations.
5. **Action:** a limited coaching adjustment with a stop or success rule.

Example: "The third 50 was 1.1 seconds slower than the second 50" is an observation. "The swimmer lacked lactate tolerance" is an unsupported physiological conclusion unless an appropriate protocol and measured data exist.

# Timing and video consistency

- Compare like definitions: head, hand, feet, wall contact, 5 m, 10 m, or 15 m are not interchangeable.
- Document whether split times are cumulative or segmental.
- Use the same camera placement, frame rate, reference marks, and start frame when comparing video.
- Treat hand timing precision realistically; do not display false hundredth-level certainty.
- For turn analysis, define the segment boundaries before interpreting the time.
- If a checkpoint is obscured or interpolated, mark it modeled and lower confidence.

# Conversion and standards boundary

A course conversion is a planning estimate. Entry eligibility depends on the specific meet, federation, qualifying window, proof rules, event, category, course, and season. LaneLab should never convert a time and announce that it is guaranteed to be accepted.

Official standards and records are date-sensitive deterministic data. Retrieval documents may explain them but may not overwrite them.
