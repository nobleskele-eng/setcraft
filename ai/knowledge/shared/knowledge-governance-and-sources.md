# Purpose

This document controls how LaneLab uses outside information. Retrieval is a support layer, not permission to copy or trust everything published online. LaneLab should prefer a small, traceable, current corpus over a large unreviewed scrape.

# Evidence levels

1. **Governing rules and official data.** Current World Aquatics, national-federation, meet, timing, and result documents. These control legal course, stroke, start, turn, entry, and record questions. Rules are date-sensitive and must be checked against the governing meet.
2. **Systematic reviews, consensus statements, and peer-reviewed research.** Use these for broad performance, monitoring, taper, strength, pacing, and injury-risk claims. Report limitations and population differences.
3. **Federation coach education.** Use for athlete development, safeguarding, coaching process, and practical teaching frameworks. Do not confuse education material with a competition rule.
4. **Qualified coach practice.** Sets, drills, cues, and progressions can be useful examples when the author, context, population, and intended outcome are known. They remain options, not universal prescriptions.
5. **Media, blogs, podcasts, YouTube, Instagram, and forums.** Use only as discovery or an attributed practitioner perspective. A viral drill, set, or race interpretation is not evidence by itself. Do not ingest comments, personal data, or entire transcripts.

# Copyright and permission rule

- Do not upload entire books, paid courses, subscription articles, social-media archives, or copied video transcripts unless LaneLab has explicit permission or a compatible licence.
- Store original LaneLab summaries, short attributed facts, bibliographic metadata, and links.
- A public URL does not mean the content is free to republish or use as a commercial training corpus.
- SwimSwam, coach interviews, podcasts, and creator channels may inform a human review queue, but their content is not automatically approved knowledge.
- Remove identifiable athlete information, especially for minors.

# Source register

The following sources informed the LaneLab baseline. The knowledge files paraphrase narrow conclusions and do not reproduce source text.

## Governing and federation sources

- World Aquatics, *Competition Regulations*, version dated 2026-02-18. Use for current international competition rules and confirm later amendments before answering a rule question. https://resources.fina.org/fina/document/2026/02/18/e6815ecc-06d9-4f0b-98e9-4c441cf5e6a3/2026-02-18_World-Aquatics_CR-Final.pdf
- USA Swimming, *American Development Model*. Use for long-term development and the competence, character, confidence, and connection framework. https://www.usaswimming.org/coaches-leaders/coaches/american-development-model
- USA Swimming, *Coach Education*. Use for the existence and scope of coach education, not as a substitute for completing certification. https://www.usaswimming.org/coaches-leaders/coaches/education
- USA Swimming, *Athlete Protection Training*. Use for safeguarding awareness; direct users to their governing organization for current requirements. https://www.usaswimming.org/resource-center/athlete-protection-training
- World Aquatics, *Warm-Up Guidelines, 10-lane long course*. Use as an example of event-specific warm-up controls; the actual meet instructions govern. https://resources.fina.org/fina/document/2026/01/22/24fc10e5-616a-4195-8987-fd0402e34f4f/Warm-Up-Guidelines-10-lane-Long-Course_26072025.pdf

## Reviews and research syntheses

- McGibbon et al., *Pacing in Swimming: A Systematic Review*. PMID 29560605. Supports treating pacing as event- and distance-dependent rather than prescribing one universal split shape. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29560605/
- Gonjo et al., *Race Analysis in Competitive Swimming: A Narrative Review*. PMID 33374118. Supports segmenting races and preserving measurement definitions and provenance. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33374118/
- Born et al., *Start and turn performances of elite male swimmers*. PMID 33663342. Supports the performance importance of start and turn segments while warning against transferring elite male benchmarks to every population. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33663342/
- Vantorre et al., *Biomechanical analysis of the swim-start: a review*. PMID 24790473. Supports analyzing block, flight, entry, underwater, and breakout as connected phases. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24790473/
- de Jesus et al., *Back plate and wedge use...: a systematic review*. PMID 35019825. Supports individualizing start setup rather than imposing one stance. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35019825/
- West et al., *Undulatory underwater kick and start/turn performance*. PMID 35900641. Supports observing underwater velocity, distance, and transition without assuming that farther is always faster. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35900641/
- Toubekis et al., *Metabolic responses relative to critical velocity*. PMID 23449237. Supports using protocol-specific critical-speed information cautiously for endurance evaluation and pace adjustment. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23449237/
- Feijen et al., *Monitoring the swimmer's training load*. PMID 32767794. Supports separating external workload from internal response and monitoring trends rather than a single score. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32767794/
- Mujika, *Quantification of training and competition loads in endurance sports*. PMID 27918666. Supports combining appropriate internal and external load measures and keeping method definitions stable. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27918666/
- Wang et al., *Effects of tapering on performance in endurance athletes: systematic review and meta-analysis*. PMID 37163550. Supports progressive volume reduction with maintained quality as a general evidence-informed option, with individualization. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37163550/
- Neiva et al., *Warm-up and performance in competitive swimming*. PMID 24178508. Supports treating warm-up as context dependent and evaluating the gap between warm-up and race. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24178508/
- Crowley et al., *Impact of resistance training on swimming performance: systematic review*. PMID 28497283. Supports well-designed strength work while avoiding guarantees of transfer. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28497283/
- Hermosilla et al., *Dry-land training programs and swimming turns: systematic review*. PMID 34501929. Supports strength/power as a possible contributor to turns when technique and transfer are also trained. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34501929/
- Barry et al., *Training load and pain, injury and illness in competitive swimmers: systematic review*. PMID 33486408. Supports conservative load monitoring; it does not permit AI injury prediction. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33486408/
- McKenzie et al., *Shoulder pain and injury risk factors in competitive swimmers: systematic review*. PMID 37515375. Supports avoiding simplistic single-cause claims about shoulder pain. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37515375/
- Price et al., *Physical performance determinants in competitive youth swimmers: systematic review*. PMID 38238793. Supports considering development, strength, power, and body composition as population-level associations, never talent judgments about one child. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38238793/
- Papadimitriou et al., *USRPT systematic review*. PMID 39195603. Supports presenting USRPT as one debated programming approach with advantages, limitations, and context—not a universal system. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39195603/
- Fone et al., *Effects of different types of strength training on swimming performance: systematic review and meta-analysis*. PMID 35099631. Supports combined swim and strength programs while requiring athlete-specific programming. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35099631/
- Shaw et al., *Nutrition for swimming*. PMID 24903758. Nutrition questions should stay general and refer individual plans to a qualified sports dietitian. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24903758/

# Review cadence

- Check competition rules before each season and before a rules-sensitive release.
- Review evidence summaries annually or when an important new systematic review appears.
- Review coach-created documents whenever the target population, equipment, or program philosophy changes.
- Retire a document when its source is withdrawn, its method cannot be reproduced, or its guidance conflicts with newer rules or stronger evidence.

# Retrieval behavior

When sources disagree, state the disagreement. When evidence is indirect, say so. When a source studied elite adults, do not silently transfer its numerical result to age-group swimmers. Prefer a coach-observable test over a confident causal story.
