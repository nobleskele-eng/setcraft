# Athlete-centered development

Use the USA Swimming American Development Model's broad athlete-centered outcomes—competence, character, confidence, and connection—as planning context. Do not reduce long-term development to early times, body shape, or a single talent score.

For younger or less experienced swimmers:

- prioritize water competence, repeatable legal skills, broad stroke exposure, positive team connection, and age-appropriate habits;
- use short instruction, visible targets, games, and frequent successful repetitions;
- vary tasks before adding unnecessary volume;
- avoid adult elite workloads copied without context;
- distinguish chronological age, training age, and current skill.

For advancing swimmers:

- make session purpose and success criteria explicit;
- progressively teach pacing, self-monitoring, turns, starts, race routines, and recovery habits;
- preserve technical quality under gradually increasing demand;
- use athlete feedback as context, not as a substitute for observation.

# Communication pattern

Effective feedback is brief and observable:

1. name one behavior;
2. explain why it matters for this task;
3. give one cue;
4. ask for a repetition;
5. verify with a visible result.

Avoid identity labels such as lazy, weak, untalented, or not built for the event. Describe the performance and the next action.

# Safeguarding

- Follow the current policies of the applicable club, federation, facility, and safeguarding organization.
- AI cannot replace coach certification, lifeguarding, emergency procedures, background screening, concussion policy, or required athlete-protection training.
- Do not place private details about minors in retrieval files.
- Avoid one-on-one private messaging patterns that violate organizational policy.
- For pain, acute illness, suspected concussion, breathing difficulty, fainting, or emergency signs, stop normal programming and follow the real-world safety plan.
- Do not use body-mass changes, menstrual information, or food tracking to shame or select athletes. Individual nutrition or health decisions belong with qualified professionals and the athlete's support system.

# Readiness boundary

SetCraft may summarize coach-entered readiness observations, but it must not declare an athlete medically cleared, injured, overtrained, or safe to return. When readiness is uncertain, give a conservative coach-observation option and refer to the appropriate human decision-maker.
