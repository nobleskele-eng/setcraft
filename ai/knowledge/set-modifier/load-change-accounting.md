# What to recalculate

For every modification, calculate:

- total distance;
- distance by stroke;
- high-intensity distance;
- expected block duration;
- work and recovery windows;
- number of starts, turns, or underwater exposures when relevant;
- equipment exposures;
- number of target-quality attempts.

# Density change

Density changes when work or recovery changes. Shorter repeats on the same send-off may create much more rest. Longer repeats with unchanged rest may shift speed work toward endurance. State the direction of change rather than pretending the set stayed identical.

# Intensity change

Do not replace prohibited equipment or stroke by increasing effort automatically. If the new movement is less specific, label the transfer limitation.

# Volume change

A smaller total can still provide the primary adaptation when quality is preserved. A larger total can dilute the purpose or add risk. Report both total and quality volume.

# Technical-load change

Track skill exposures separately from metres:

- number of legal turns;
- timed starts or 15 m segments;
- race-pace breakouts;
- full-stroke butterfly cycles;
- breaststroke pullouts;
- high-speed finishes.

# Internal response

RPE, session feedback, and wellness are subjective context. Do not convert them into a diagnosis. Compare trends using the same method and allow the coach to adjust the next exposure.

# Example accounting statement

"The revision preserves the aerobic purpose and 24 minutes of controlled work, lowers total distance from 2,400 to 2,100, removes paddles and butterfly, increases recovery by approximately 10–15 seconds per repeat for this lane, and changes the technical stimulus from butterfly rhythm to permitted freestyle body-line control. Coach check: confirm the entered shoulder restriction permits the selected stroke and stop the block if the real-world safety plan requires it."

# Evidence boundary

Training-load literature supports monitoring exposure and response trends, but it does not give LaneLab permission to predict injury in an individual. Source anchors: PMID 32767794, PMID 33486408, and PMID 37515375.
