# Mixed ability in one lane

Preserve shared start/finish times with different distances or use pace bands with staggered starts. Avoid a single send-off that leaves one athlete no recovery and another excessive recovery.

Report:

- A/B/C distance or repetition options;
- expected work and recovery windows;
- shared success measure;
- lane-order and passing plan.

# Shorter practice time

Keep the primary purpose and remove low-priority volume first. Compressing rest may change the adaptation, so do not shrink a 90-minute session into 60 minutes by proportionally shortening every send-off.

Priority order:

1. safety and preparation;
2. primary quality block;
3. necessary recovery;
4. secondary work;
5. optional volume.

# No equipment

Replace the function, not the object:

- fins for velocity/body-line support -> shorter assisted-quality distance is unavailable, so use shorter unassisted repetitions with more recovery and a line cue;
- paddles for force/connection -> use scull, fist-to-swim contrast, resistance-free connection cues, or dryland only if appropriate;
- snorkel for stable head/breath removal -> use limited breathing pattern without unsafe breath holding, or shorter non-breathing technical segments under coach supervision;
- pull buoy for body position -> use body-line cues, controlled kick, or shorter repetitions.

State that the stimulus is not identical.

# Shoulder-related coach-entered restriction

Treat it as a hard constraint, not a diagnosis. Remove the prohibited stroke/equipment/movement. Preserve aerobic purpose with permitted lower-load swimming, kick, or time-based alternatives only if the coach has entered them as allowed. Avoid claiming medical safety. Refer readiness and return decisions to the appropriate professional.

# Knee-related coach-entered restriction

Do not assume which kick is permitted. Ask or follow entered constraints. Breaststroke kick may be excluded, but AI must not infer a diagnosis. Preserve the session with permitted strokes or upper-body work only when explicitly allowed.

# Lower-back-related coach-entered restriction

Do not infer the cause. Respect entered exclusions for dolphin kick, starts, resisted work, or body positions. Offer neutral alternatives only within those coach-entered limits.

# Beginner adaptation

Shorten instruction and repeat distance, increase feedback, reduce simultaneous demands, use clear legal-skill goals, and create frequent successes. Preserve the concept while removing advanced pace precision that the swimmer cannot yet execute.

# Senior/advanced adaptation

Increase specificity through verified pace bands, segment timing, technical metrics, or controlled complexity. More volume is not the default progression.

# Recovery-day adaptation

Reduce intensity and competitive pressure. Keep movement quality and easy technical rehearsal. Do not preserve a hard set's distance by labeling it recovery.

# Meet-week adaptation

Reduce unnecessary load, preserve familiar quality and event skills, and avoid new equipment or unfamiliar maximal testing. Individual taper plans should reflect prior response.

# Overcrowded lane adaptation

Prefer time-based blocks, shorter segments, offset starts, clear overtaking rules, and synchronized recovery. Avoid maximal-speed work when traffic prevents safe, valid timing.

# Course change

Recalculate distances, turns, and target splits. Do not simply relabel metres as yards. A planning conversion is not an official entry result.
