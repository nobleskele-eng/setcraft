# Build around the lane that exists

A theoretically elegant set fails if the lane cannot execute it. Before assigning, account for swimmer count, speed range, passing risk, equipment changes, pool configuration, staffing, and actual completion times.

# Send-off design

- Choose the work duration and recovery demanded by the objective.
- Check the slowest assigned swimmer's expected completion time.
- If faster swimmers receive excessive rest, use pace targets, subgroups, staggered starts, or a different repeat length.
- Avoid send-offs that force constant passing during technical or race-pace work.
- For mixed lanes, preserve the same purpose with different distances, cycle counts, or repetitions.

# Useful differentiation patterns

- **Same time, different distance:** useful when lanes must finish together.
- **Same distance, different send-off:** useful when space and speed groups permit.
- **Same purpose, different stroke:** useful with a coach-entered restriction or development need.
- **Same block, different success rule:** useful when athletes share the set but have individualized targets.
- **Choice within guardrails:** useful for autonomy when all options serve the same objective.

# Quality controls

Examples of observable controls:

- pace window;
- maximum fall-off from best successful repetition;
- stroke-count band at a stated speed;
- breakout distance or 15 m time;
- turn-segment time;
- legal-skill success rate;
- technical rating with a defined rubric;
- number of successful repetitions before progression.

When a quality control fails, the coach may add recovery, shorten the repeat, lower intensity, switch to a technical reset, or stop the block. The correct response depends on the intended adaptation.

# Feedback rhythm

- Watch at least one full attempt before changing the task.
- Give one primary cue.
- Allow enough repetitions for the athlete to explore it.
- Ask the athlete what changed.
- Confirm with the chosen measure.
- Store a short note for the next session.

# Group climate

Use competition selectively. A ranking that rewards only the fastest swimmer can undermine technical or developmental goals. Alternatives include improvement from baseline, team consistency, legal-skill percentage, or hitting an individual pace band.

# Session debrief

Record:

- objective achieved, partly achieved, or missed;
- best evidence;
- where lane logistics changed the dose;
- athlete feedback themes;
- restrictions or follow-up items;
- one change for the next exposure.
